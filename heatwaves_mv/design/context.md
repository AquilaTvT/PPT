## Meaning
Glass Animals' "Heat Waves" is about late June nights replaying a lost love: memories that look like a mirage on a hot road
(road shimmer, swimming in a mirror), knowing you can't make the other person happier now, and having to let go.
Lime's Japanese lyrics keep that: 光りだすロード / 揺らいでくビジョン (the road starting to shine, the vision wavering),
熱波ゆらり 泳いでくミラー (heat waves sway, swimming in the mirror), 思い浮かぶの君ばかり (all I think of is you),
初夏零時過ぎたばかり (just past midnight in early summer), ヒートウェイブに迷うのさ (lost in the heat wave),
君に幸せあげられない (I can't give you happiness); verse: keeping the TV on so as not to think of us, today's Hollywood
movie is like a projection of us, you'd be happy if it weren't me, 逃げ水 spreading on the road, no longer visible in the
dark; bridge: can't fight, can't breathe, even if we said love I should go, someone else suits you better, it hurts but
you know it too, you look like you'll break while crying, one more time before goodbye; bridge 2: what dream are you in,
smiling contentedly, realizing my own powerlessness, facing your unclouded face.

## Production capabilities (GPU, ComfyUI) — design for these
- Keyframes: anime illustrations from Anima (anime model, Danbooru tags + natural language), 1536x864 or similar,
  plus character LoRAs trained for the two leads (consistent face/hair/outfits), Qwen-Image-Edit 2511 for
  identity-preserving variations (same character, new pose/angle/outfit/lighting), Anima ControlNet-lite (canny/depth/pose)
  for exact composition.
- Video: image-to-video (Wan 2.2 A14B or HunyuanVideo 1.5) at 720p, upscaled to 1080p (SeedVR2), interpolated to 30 fps.
  One generation = up to ~5 s (81 frames at 16 fps or 121 frames at 24 fps). Longer shots = chain clips (last frame ->
  next first frame) or first-last-frame (FLF) generation between two keyframes (great for morphs/transitions).
  Works well: hair/clothes in wind, subtle acting (blink, look up, smile, tears, turning the head), walking/running,
  camera dolly/orbit/crane/pan, environmental motion (clouds, water, fireworks, trains passing, light changes, rain,
  heat shimmer), particles. Works poorly: precise hand-object interaction, readable text inside the video,
  fast complex choreography, more than two interacting characters, very long continuous takes.
- Compositing (Python): kinetic typography (Japanese vertical/horizontal, Chinese translation), transitions (match cuts,
  morph via FLF, wipes, light leaks, dissolves, glitch, heat-haze distortions), grading, particles, split screens,
  layering cut-out characters (SAM3 video segmentation), speed ramps, multi-layer parallax.
- Optional: Blender 3D scenes rendered on the GPU.
- Budget guide: ~60–90 generated clips per MV is feasible.

## Client requirements (non-negotiable)
- Two separate MVs. MV-A: free creative direction, the highest quality possible. MV-B: the pixel-like style of the
  reference 千本桜 MV (a separate brief).
- Every shot must be genuinely animated (characters/environment actually move). NO shots that are just a still
  image slowly zooming/panning. NO crude black silhouettes for people — characters are fully drawn and animated.
- Storyboard must be coherent and flow smoothly; cuts and motion synced to the music.
- Must clearly surpass the reference MV in quality.
